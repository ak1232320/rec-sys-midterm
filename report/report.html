Familiar Programmes, Counted: Weighted ALS and Rank Fusion for sweet.tv TV Recommendation

Student: Aleksei Kosychev    Team: Individual    Email: amkosychev@edu.hse.ru    Date: 2026-10-23
Midterm quiz (GSB): TV programme recommendation on sweet.tv, Kaggle MAP@5    Code and submission files: github.com/ak1232320/rec-sys-midterm

Abstract

I join 5.8 M views to the broadcast schedule and predict each of 1,260 users' five most-preferred programmes for weeks 31-42. Two submissions that differ only in the ranking signal show that the target behaves like the number of airings a user watched ≥ 80% of in one view (n80). I use that definition for validation: fit on weeks 11-20, score on 21-30.

On that split, a weighted ALS that regresses log n80 is the best single method, at MAP@5 0.324. It is followed by Personalized PageRank (0.319), implicit ALS (0.314), history replay (0.308), association rules (0.277), popularity (0.223) and CBF (0.185). A rank fusion of four methods adds +0.003 on held-out users (95% CI [0.001, 0.005]) and is best in every activity quartile.

The order carries over to the test window. Kaggle public scores are 0.4265 for the hybrid, 0.4191 for weighted ALS, 0.4084 for PageRank and 0.3960 for replay. About 95% of what every history-based method recommends is already familiar to the user: this platform's preferences are mostly habits.

1. Data and interactions

The data. The view log has 5,821,987 views by 4,914 users on 202 channels in ISO weeks 11-30 of 2020. All 1,260 test users appear in it. Only 10.8% of schedule rows (16-17% of airtime) carry a programme id. Labels follow the category: entertainment, films, series and children's shows are 44-86% labelled, sport and music almost never. Yet 56% of viewing seconds land on labelled airings.

The join. A view matches an airing when they are on the same channel and their intervals overlap. Both sources use local wall-clock time. The 117 views whose duraton differs from stop − start differ by exactly 3,600 s, and they are exactly the views spanning the 2020-03-29 switch to summer time. View stops cluster at programme boundaries only when no shift is applied.

Three adjustments follow:

  • five airings that overrun the next one are cut at its start;
  • 12% of views overlap another view of the same account, so a user's time on an airing is the union of their intervals, not the sum;
  • tv_show_id = 0 is unlabelled airtime and never an item.

What counts as watched. No view in the log is shorter than 61 s, so zapping has already been removed. Overlaps under 60 s are only the edges of views that straddle a boundary (2.5% of user × airing pairs, 0.05% of seconds), so 60 s is the floor for any interaction. The share of an airing that a user covers is U-shaped: 27% of pairs cover under 5% and 16% cover at least 95%. A search-engine summary of the 2nd-place write-up [1] quotes the organisers' rule as "over 80% of the programme without changing the channel", which defines n80.

Which target. Ranking by seconds, by airings ≥ 60 s or by n80 gives different top-5 lists: an oracle of one definition scores only 0.46-0.57 MAP@5 against another. I submitted history replay twice, ranked once by seconds and once by n80:

Two log defects. From 17 Jun to 2 Jul the number of users per day halves, and 3 Jul is missing. Sessions longer than 4 h make up 1.4-3.3% of views in weeks 19-24 and 27-30, but at most 0.04% in weeks 11-18. n80 counts airings rather than seconds, which makes it less exposed to both.

2. Methods

Shared protocol.

  • Every method sees only the history weeks.
  • The candidate pool is the labelled programmes in the target window's schedule (6,363 for 21-30, 6,636 for 31-42). The test schedule is used only to know what airs.
  • Repeats are allowed, because 67% of next-window top-5 items were already in the user's history.
  • One shared function turns any score matrix into exactly five distinct ids, breaking ties by popularity. A user without usable history gets an all-zero row, and therefore the popularity list from every method. This applies to two test users.

Methods.

  • Popularity (required baseline): programmes ordered by the number of users with an n80 interaction.
  • History replay (reference only): the user's programmes by n80, then by seconds.
  • MF (a): implicit ALS [2] on n80, confidence 1 + α·n80, using implicit 0.7.3.
  • MF (b): weighted ALS on graded counts, my own implementation. It minimises Σ c(r − x_u·y_i)² + λ‖·‖² with r = log(1 + n80) and c = 1 + α·r, with target 0 and weight 1 on unobserved cells. Variant (a) learns whether a user watches; this one keeps how often.
  • Personalized PageRank [3] on the programme co-view graph:
    • edge weight = users with n80 on both programmes, no self-loops;
    • restart probability β at the user's n80 vector r_u;
    • π_u = β r_u (I − (1−β)P)^{-1}, solved exactly. One inverse, at most 7.9k × 7.9k, serves all users in a few seconds.
  • Association rules across time [4]. x → y counts users with x in the first half of the history and y in the second, and x → x measures how often a programme's audience returns. The score is the highest confidence among rules whose antecedent is in the user's recent half.
  • CBF [5]: TF-IDF over category, genres, decade, directors and actors (names shared by ≥ 2 programmes), with field weights. The profile is the n80-weighted sum and the score is the cosine.
  • Hybrid: rank fusion [6]. Score = Σ_m w_m / (c + rank_m) over each method's top 100. Ranks are used because the scores share no scale.

Validation. Methods are fit on weeks 11-20 and scored on each user's n80 top-5 in weeks 21-30, against the 21-30 candidates. The metric is MAP@5 over the 4,623 users with a non-empty truth, of whom 1,236 are test users. Hyperparameters are chosen on all users (grids in validation.json). The hybrid's weights and c are chosen by coordinate ascent on one half of the users and scored on the other (2-fold, seed 42).

For the submissions, everything is refit on weeks 11-20 + 21-30. Longer history helps: PageRank on 5, 8 and 10 weeks scores 0.303, 0.314 and 0.319. The environment and runtimes are in the repository README.

4. Business and algorithmic analysis: which strategy, and why

My recommendation: the rank-fusion hybrid, led by weighted ALS. Weighted ALS is the best single method on validation, in every activity quartile, and on Kaggle (0.4191). Adding association rules, PageRank and CBF raises the held-out score by a small but significant 0.003. It also raises the public score to 0.4265. Its private score is 0.4221; the frozen leaderboard's best private score is 0.4291. Kaggle ranks the four submitted methods exactly as validation did, which suggests the 11-20 → 21-30 protocol is a fair stand-in for the test.

The whole pipeline costs little: all methods refit in under a minute (50 s in the recorded run) on a laptop for 4,914 users × 7,886 programmes. PageRank's exact inverse grows with the cube of the catalogue, so at a larger scale it would switch to power iteration on the sparse graph.

Familiar programmes, not discovery. In the hybrid's lists for test users, 95% of the items are programmes the user already watched. Their precision is 0.43, against 0.094 for the 5% that are new, or 0.024 new hits per user.

The lists therefore work as a "your programmes, airing now" shelf. They keep the morning show, the evening news and a running series in view. They are not a discovery feed.

An unfamiliar programme is not automatically a discovery. CBF fills 32% of its slots with unseen programmes, yet only 1.2% of those turn out to be among the user's next favourites. Nothing in the data says whether a hit was enjoyed. And "unwatched" only means unwatched on this platform.

How the methods differ.

  • Association rules do something MF does not: they score each programme's return rate explicitly. The median confidence of x → x (weeks 11-15 → 16-20, programmes with ≥ 20 viewers) is 0.30 for news, 0.26 for children's, 0.18 for series and 0.00 for films. MF folds this into a user's taste vector instead. With a support threshold of 20 the rules are popularity-bound: they reach only 25 distinct programmes across all test users. They do best for light viewers (0.156 in the bottom quartile, level with weighted ALS), which is where they help the fusion most.
  • PageRank's diffusion barely changes the composition of the lists: 97.6% of PageRank's items are familiar, against 99.2% for replay. It changes the order. Familiar programmes are ranked by how strongly they connect to the rest of the user's history, which raises their precision from 0.400 to 0.412, and short lists are completed with co-viewed programmes instead of the global top five. It gains +0.011 over replay, and every β ≥ 0.3 lands within 0.004.
  • Content attributes are the only signal for programmes nobody has watched. 22% of the test window's ids are new, and 89% of those are films. In practice, though, CBF finds programmes with the same cast: the actor weight of 16 wins. On its own CBF scores lowest, and it earns a small weight in the fusion.
  • Popularity is already strong where history is thin. For the lightest quartile it beats the user's own replay (0.140 against 0.129), because the top list is five news programmes that most households watch.

The two users. For the heavy viewer, replay, both MF variants, PageRank and the hybrid recover three of the five real favourites (Факты, ТСН, Завтрак с 1+1); the rules and CBF recover two. The misses, Гражданская оборона and Гримм, were in the history but ranked below fifth.

The light viewer is the counter-example. Replay hits two (Мир наизнанку, CSI), while weighted ALS and the hybrid hit none: they smooth this user toward the news that similar users watch. The quartile table shows that this is the exception and not the rule. Three of this user's five real favourites were films they had never watched, which no history-based method could have ranked.

Limitations the results support.

  • (i) Only 10.8% of schedule rows carry an id. 44% of viewing seconds (sport, music, "other") is invisible to every method, and so are users whose habits live there.
  • (ii) The target definition is inferred. The probe separates three candidates, but a close variant of n80 is not excluded.
  • (iii) The log has recording gaps (17 Jun - 3 Jul) and changing session-length behaviour. Validation is 10 → 10 weeks, while the submission is 20 → 12 weeks.
  • (iv) The gain of the hybrid over weighted ALS is small. It is significant on validation, but one Kaggle submission per method cannot show significance on the test.
  • (v) No online experiment was run, and no claim about engagement or revenue follows from this offline evaluation.
